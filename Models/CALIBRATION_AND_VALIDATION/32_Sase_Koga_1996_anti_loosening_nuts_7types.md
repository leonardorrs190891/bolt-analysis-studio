# Study 32: Sase, Koga, Nishioka & Fujii (1996) — Evaluation of Anti-Loosening Nuts (7 Types)

## Full Citation
**Authors**: Sase, N.; Koga, S.; Nishioka, K.; Fujii, H.
**Title**: "Evaluation of anti-loosening nuts for screw fasteners"
**Journal**: Journal of Materials Processing Technology, 1996, 56(1–4), 321–332
**DOI**: 10.1016/0924-0136(95)01846-8

---

## Significance
One of the earliest systematic comparisons of anti-loosening nut types. Tests **seven device types** under both forced transverse displacement and high-acceleration shaking (up to 30 g). Includes types rarely tested elsewhere: U-nut and edged spring nut.

---

## Experimental Setup

### Test Methods

#### Method A: Forced Transverse Displacement (Junker-type)
- **Amplitude**: ±0.3 mm to ±1.0 mm (stepped)
- **Frequency**: 10 Hz
- **Duration**: 3,000 cycles per amplitude step
- **Bolt**: M10 × 1.5, JIS Grade 4.8 and 10.9

#### Method B: High-Acceleration Shaking
- **Excitation**: Sinusoidal, 50–500 Hz sweep
- **Acceleration**: Up to 30 g
- **Bolt**: M10 × 1.5, mounted on shaker table

### Bolt Specifications
- **Size**: M10 × 1.5
- **Grades tested**: 4.8 (low strength) and 10.9 (high strength)
- **Preload**: 70% of proof load
  - Grade 4.8: F₀ = 16.8 kN
  - Grade 10.9: F₀ = 40.6 kN

### Anti-Loosening Devices Tested

| # | Device | Type | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Standard hex nut | Reference | Friction only |
| 2 | Nylon insert nut (DIN 985) | Prevailing torque | Nylon deforms onto threads |
| 3 | All-metal insert nut | Prevailing torque | Crimped metal crown |
| 4 | Edged spring nut | Friction enhancement | Serrated bearing face |
| 5 | Serrated flange nut | Friction enhancement | Teeth on flange face |
| 6 | Double nut (proper) | Opposing threads | Upper nut loaded in tension |
| 7 | U-nut (speed nut) | Spring action | Sheet metal spring clip |

## Key Findings

1. **Prevailing torque nuts (nylon, metal) cannot stop loosening** — they only slow it slightly
2. **Double nut is most effective** but requires correct installation (thin nut first, then thick)
3. **U-nut** (sheet metal clip) surprisingly effective — nearly as good as double nut
4. **Serrated flange nut** provides moderate resistance by increasing effective friction
5. **Edged spring nut** is similar to serrated flange — friction enhancement only
6. **Bolt grade matters** — higher grade = higher absolute preload = better resistance
