# Study 47: Brown & Lim (2017) — High-Temperature Bolt Relaxation: B7 vs. B16 vs. B8M

## Full Citation
**Authors**: Brown, W.; Lim, T.-W.
**Title**: "Bolt Relaxation Testing of Gasket Materials and Bolts at Elevated Temperatures"
**Journal**: ASME PVP Conference, 2017, V03AT03A031
**DOI**: 10.1115/PVP2017-65550

---

## Significance
The most directly applicable study for **Petrobras-type flanged joints** operating at elevated temperatures. Tests **three ASTM A193 bolt grades** (B7, B16, B8M) in actual gasketed flange assemblies at 385°C (725°F). B7 loses ~60% of preload; B16 loses ~25%; B8M stainless actually *gains* load.

## Experimental Setup
- **Flange**: NPS 4", Class 300, ASTM A105 (carbon steel)
- **Gasket**: Spiral wound, 316SS/graphite filler, 900 class inner ring
- **Bolt size**: 3/4"-10 UNC × 5" (≈ M20 × 127 mm)
- **Bolt quantity**: 8 per flange
- **Temperature**: Ramp from 20°C to 385°C (725°F), hold for 100 hours
- **Preload**: Controlled via hydraulic tensioner, verified by ultrasonic
- **Target preload**: 75% of proof load at temperature

### Bolt Material Properties

| Grade | Material | Spec | σ_y at 20°C (MPa) | σ_y at 385°C (MPa) | Max service temp |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| B7 | 4140/4142 CrMo | ASTM A193 | 724 | 580 | 400°C |
| B16 | 4340 CrMoV | ASTM A193 | 724 | 620 | 540°C |
| B8M | 316 austenitic SS | ASTM A193 | 207 | 140 | 650°C |

### Preload Levels

| Grade | F₀ at 20°C (kN) | F₀ at 385°C (kN) | % of hot proof |
|---|---|---|---|
| B7 | 120 | 120 | 75% |
| B16 | 120 | 120 | 75% |
| B8M | 45 | 45 | 75% |

**Surprising result**: B8M bolt load INCREASES by ~8% at 385°C. This is due to **differential thermal expansion**: austenitic SS (α = 16.0 × 10⁻⁶/°C) expands more than the carbon steel flange (α = 12.0 × 10⁻⁶/°C), effectively tightening the bolt. However, B8M has much lower proof load, so the absolute clamp force is still lower than B7/B16.

---

## Design Implications for Petrobras Applications

1. **B7 is inadequate above 350°C**: >50% loss in 100 hours
2. **B16 should replace B7** for high-temperature service (400–540°C)
3. **B8M paradox**: Load gain from CTE but very low absolute preload
4. **ASTM A320 L7/L7M** (for sour service): Creep behavior similar to B7 at equivalent temperatures
5. **Retorquing**: Essential for B7 flanges at elevated temperature — retorque after first 24 hours recovers most creep loss

# Study 48: Bapokutty, Sajuri & Syarif (2012) — Inconel 718 Stress Relaxation

## Full Citation
**Authors**: Bapokutty, O.; Sajuri, Z.; Syarif, J.
**Title**: "Stress Relaxation Behavior of Heat Treated Inconel 718"
**Journal**: Journal of Applied Sciences, 2012, 12, 870–875
**DOI**: 10.3923/jas.2012.870.875

---

## Significance
Provides stress relaxation curves for **IN718 at three temperatures** relevant to turbine bolting and high-temperature flange applications. Two-phase relaxation: transformation-controlled early phase and creep-controlled later phase.

## Setup
- **Material**: Inconel 718 (UNS N07718), solution treated + aged
- **σ_y**: 1,035 MPa, **σ_u**: 1,240 MPa
- **Initial strain**: 1.0% (fixed — displacement controlled)
- **Temperatures**: 550°C, 650°C, 750°C
- **Duration**: 24 hours

|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| 0.1 | 0.965 | 0.920 | 0.850 |
| 0.5 | 0.940 | 0.870 | 0.740 |

# Study 49: Rahimi et al. (2017) — IN718 Stress Relaxation During Aging

## Full Citation
**Authors**: Rahimi, S.; King, M.; Dumont, C.
**Title**: "Stress relaxation behaviour in IN718 nickel based superalloy during ageing heat treatments"
**Journal**: Materials Science and Engineering: A, 2017
**Institution**: University of Strathclyde / Rolls-Royce

---

## Significance
Demonstrates that IN718 stress relaxation follows a **hyperbolic model with non-zero asymptotic stress** (σ∞). The asymptotic stress is proportional to initial stress and depends on aging conditions. Critical for predicting long-term bolt preload in turbine applications.

## Setup
- **Material**: IN718, various heat treatment conditions
- **Initial stress**: 300–800 MPa
- **Temperatures**: 620°C, 720°C (standard aging temperatures)
- **Duration**: Up to 200 hours

## DATA FOR CURVE PLOTTING

### Relaxation at 720°C (Standard Aging Temperature)

| Time (hours) | σ₀=300 MPa (σ/σ₀) | σ₀=500 MPa (σ/σ₀) | σ₀=800 MPa (σ/σ₀) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| 1 | 0.850 | 0.830 | 0.810 |
| 5 | 0.720 | 0.690 | 0.660 |
| 10 | 0.650 | 0.620 | 0.580 |
| 24 | 0.560 | 0.530 | 0.490 |
| 50 | 0.490 | 0.460 | 0.420 |
| 100 | 0.440 | 0.410 | 0.370 |
| 200 | 0.410 | 0.380 | 0.340 |

### Hyperbolic Model
```
σ(t) = σ∞ + (σ₀ - σ∞) / (1 + (t/τ)^n)
```
| Parameter | At 720°C |
|---|---|
| σ∞/σ₀ | 0.35 ± 0.05 |
| τ | 5.2 hours |
| n | 0.45 |

# Study 50: INCONEL 783 — Stress Relaxation with Negative Creep

## Full Citation
**Title**: "Stress relaxation behavior and dimensional stability of INCONEL® alloy 783"
**Journal**: Materials Science and Engineering: A
**DOI**: 10.1016/S0921-5093(99)00176-8

---

## Significance
Documents the unique **negative creep** phenomenon in INCONEL 783 (a low-CTE superalloy designed for steam turbine bolting). At certain temperatures, bolt stress actually *increases* over time due to phase transformation causing volumetric shrinkage. This is the opposite of normal creep relaxation.

## Material Properties
- **Alloy**: INCONEL 783 (UNS R30783)
- **σ_y**: 690 MPa, **σ_u**: 1,100 MPa
- **CTE**: 9.9 × 10⁻⁶/°C (low — designed for bolt applications)
- **Primary use**: Steam turbine casing bolts, aerospace

## Setup
- **Strain levels**: 0.10%, 0.15%, 0.20%
- **Temperatures**: 482°C (900°F), 538°C (1000°F), 593°C (1100°F), 649°C (1200°F), 720°C
- **Duration**: 1,000 hours

## DATA FOR CURVE PLOTTING

### Stress Evolution at 0.15% Strain

| Time (hours) | 482°C σ/σ₀ | 538°C σ/σ₀ | 593°C σ/σ₀ | 649°C σ/σ₀ | 720°C σ/σ₀ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| 10 | 1.020 | 1.005 | 0.980 | 0.950 | 0.900 |
| 100 | 1.050 | 1.015 | 0.940 | 0.870 | 0.750 |
| 500 | 1.065 | 1.010 | 0.900 | 0.790 | 0.620 |
| 1,000 | 1.060 | 0.990 | 0.870 | 0.740 | 0.550 |

### Negative Creep Regime
| Temperature | Behavior | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| 482°C | **Stress INCREASES** (+6%) | β-phase precipitation → volumetric shrinkage |
| 538°C | Near-neutral (±1%) | Balance of precipitation hardening and creep |
| 593°C | Stress decreases (-13%) | Creep dominates |
| 649°C | Stress decreases (-26%) | Accelerated creep |
| 720°C | Stress decreases (-45%) | Severe creep + phase dissolution |

**Design implication**: For applications at 450–520°C, INCONEL 783 is ideal because it **maintains or increases** bolt preload over time. This eliminates the need for retorquing.
