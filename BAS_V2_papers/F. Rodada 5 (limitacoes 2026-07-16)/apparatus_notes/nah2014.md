# Nah, Lee & Choi 2014 (Steel and Composite Structures) — long-term relaxation of HS bolts, faying-surface coating creep

**Citation:** Hwan-Seon Nah, Hyeon-Ju Lee, Sung-Mo Choi, "Evaluating long-term relaxation of
high strength bolts considering coating on slip faying surface," *Steel and Composite
Structures*, Vol. 16, No. 6 (2014), pp. 703–718.
**DOI:** [10.12989/scs.2014.16.6.703](https://doi.org/10.12989/scs.2014.16.6.703)
**PDF:** `Long-term relaxation of high strength bolts w coating on faying surface (Nah 2014 SCS) [L5].pdf`
(this folder)

## Target limitation: L5 — per-tribological-pair contact creep/relaxation constant `C_creep`, beyond 304SS

BAS V2's `JointMaterial.C_creep` is currently a single global default (5e-11) with only one
outside anchor (304SS thread creep, `MODEL_LEGITIMACY.md` §4.7 — disjoint from the âncora interna-fit
value, confirming `C_creep` is **per-pair, not universal**). This paper supplies **seven**
more per-pair creep-relaxation data points — all on **structural-steel faying (contact)
surfaces**, not thread/bearing interfaces — spanning two bolt coatings (dacro / plain / ASTM
A490), two paint families (inorganic zinc, red lead), and a 4-point coating-thickness sweep
(96/128/168/226 µm) within one bolt/coating pair. It is direct, quantitative evidence that a
**single creep constant is not transferable even within "steel-on-steel with a thin
protective coating"** — thickness alone changes the effective creep rate several-fold. See
"V2 mapping" below for why this is a *new pair class* (faying-surface coating creep) rather
than a re-calibration of BAS V2's existing thread/bearing `C_creep`.

## What it is (experiment)

A **static long-term relaxation test** (no cyclic/vibratory loading, no external service
load) — bolts are tightened once to an initial clamp force and then simply left standing
indoors for up to 744–1000 hours while clamp force is logged continuously. The goal is to
isolate and quantify **creep of the faying-surface coating** (paint/primer sandwiched between
the two clamped steel plates) as the dominant *long-term* (beyond the first few hours)
relaxation mechanism, as distinct from the fast elastic/embedding relaxation that happens
within the first tightening. The paper fits a **logarithmic-in-time regression** to the
measured coating creep strain, plugs it into a spring-compliance (bolt + coating in series)
force-balance model adapted from Arimond & Raymond (1996), and produces a predicted
`F(t)/F₀` curve per coating condition.

## Apparatus / method

- **Joint layout:** standard 3-plate lap joint (1 main plate + 2 lap/splice plates) built to
  Architectural Institute of Japan (2003) slip-critical bolted-connection guidelines.
  Base plate 420×100 mm (24 mm bolt hole), splice plate 350×100 mm (22 mm bolt hole), bolt
  pitch 70 mm, edge distance ≥50 mm, bolt diameter 20 mm (M20).
- **Clamp-force measurement:** strain gages bonded directly to each bolt shank (primary
  method, all specimens); washer-type load cells additionally used on some specimens.
  Load-cell sensitivity 1.345 mV/V, resistance 350 Ω ± 1% (Korean domestic manufacturer).
  Data loggers: Tokyo Sokki Kenkyujo Co., wired continuously to the shank strain gages.
- **Loading protocol:** bolts tightened once to the initial clamp force, then held static
  (no re-tightening, no cyclic load, no imposed external tension) — a pure creep/relaxation
  hold, not a fatigue or self-loosening (vibration) test.
- **Two test campaigns, 13 months apart, different ambient conditions:**
  - **Joint group-1** (inorganic-zinc thickness sweep): "room temperature/humidity
    conditions" — i.e., **ambient, uncontrolled**, subject to daily temperature
    fluctuation (explicitly noted as a source of the oscillation seen in the raw curves).
  - **Joint group-2** (thickness/species/bolt-species comparison): mixed — one specimen
    (R2KFP) run under **constant temperature/humidity**, the rest ambient/indoor.
- **Test duration:** 744 h for group-1, 1000 h for group-2 (per text; Table 2 analysis
  itself is truncated to 744 h "to accommodate consistency" between the two groups; Fig. 11
  (128 µm) shows real data points out to ≈830 h with the fitted line extrapolated flat to
  1000 h).
- **Regression tool:** MINITAB, one-way ANOVA + curvilinear regression, per coating
  condition, on **coating strain vs. elapsed time** (not directly on `F/F₀`).

## Materials

- **Primary bolt (group-1, and part of group-2):** dacro-coated (non-electrolytic zinc-flake)
  tension-control (TC) bolt, grade **F10T M20** per **KS B 2819** (Korean Standard).
  Tensile strength 1000–1200 N/mm², yield strength 900 N/mm². (No numeric preload/`F₀` or
  plate-grip-thickness value is stated anywhere in the paper — see Caveats.)
- **Comparison bolts (group-2 only):** plain (uncoated-body) tension-control bolt; **ASTM
  A490** bolt. Both nominally F10T-class per the group-2 description.
- **Plate:** **KS SM490A** structural steel (explicit for group-2; group-1 presumably the
  same class, not restated).
- **Faying-surface coatings (the actual experimental variable):**
  - **Inorganic zinc primer** — group-1 thickness sweep, required/actual (workmanship
    variance) 120/**96**, 180/**168**, 240/**226** µm; group-2 single point 120/**128** µm.
    All regression equations and figures in the paper are captioned/labeled with the
    **actual (as-built)** thickness, which is what this note and the CSVs use throughout.
  - **Red lead paint** — group-2 only: 50/**65** µm (R2KFP, plain TC bolt, climate-controlled),
    120/**125** µm ×2 (R2KDFP: dacro TC bolt; R2AFP: ASTM A490 bolt — both ambient).
  - Korean-manufacturer guideline cited: red lead paint ≤50 µm, inorganic zinc primer ≤75 µm.

### Master force-relaxation model (Eq. 4, general; Eqs. 9/11/13/15 per thickness)

```
F(t) = F₀ · [ Cf + (H₀/Ac)·α ] / [ Cf + (H₀/Ac)·(α + β·log₁₀ t) ]
Cf = ΣT_plate / (A_bolt · E_bolt)                          (Eq. 2, bolt compliance)
Ac = π/4·[(d_b + T/10)² − d_h²]     (Bickford 1998 form, Eq. 6, used here since Dⱼ>3d_b, T≤8d)
```
`Cf` = bolt compliance, `H₀` = coating Young's modulus, `Ac` = bolt stress area, `α,β` from
the table above. **`Cf`, `H₀`, `Ac` are not given as numeric values for these specimens
anywhere in the paper** — only the general formulas (Eqs. 2, 5, 6). See "Curve/table
inventory" and "Caveats" for how the predicted `F(t)/F₀` curves were nonetheless
reconstructed for the CSVs.

A **different prior-study model** is also reproduced for context (Yang & DeWolf 1999/2000,
Eq. 7, cited not fitted here): `1/Ec = α + β·tᵐ` (power-law in raw time, **not** log-time),
with `m=0.2`, `α=1.55×10⁻³`, `β=7.30×10⁻³` (claimed accuracy ±0.3%). Nah et al.'s own fit uses
a **log₁₀(t)** form instead — a form-choice divergence worth noting for V2 (see V2 mapping).

## Nuances

- **Fast first-week dominance:** relaxation in the first 168 h (7 days) is **>85%** of the
  total 744 h relaxation for the inorganic-zinc series (91–95%, except R2KDZ3-2 at 87%) —
  matches Kulak et al. (2001)'s general finding, cited approvingly.
  "Loss essentially stops" is a matter of the log(t) tail flattening, not a hard floor.
- **Monotonic thickness dependence:** loss rises monotonically with inorganic-zinc thickness
  within group-1 (96→10.9%, 168→15.7%, 226→18.1% at 744 h) — a clean, controlled sweep since
  all three share bolt/coating species and test timing.
- **Predicted-vs-test "synchronization" times differ by coating:** the smooth log-model
  systematically *lags* the real (faster) initial drop, then the gap narrows and the two
  traces cross/merge at a coating-dependent time: **~500 h** (96 µm), **~200 h** (168 µm,
  "sudden drop... roughly 4 days" vs. model), **~100 h** (128 µm, "sudden drop... 9.3% by 24
  hours"). No explicit synchronization time is stated for 226 µm; the digitized traces there
  track closely (noisy test oscillating around the smooth predicted line) through most of
  the curve.
- **Within-condition replicate scatter is real and non-trivial:** e.g., R2KDZ3-1 vs.
  R2KDZ3-2 (both 226 µm, same batch) differ by up to ~2.6 pp at 744 h (18.3% vs. 17.9%, and
  their 168h/744h ratios differ 91% vs. 87%) — only two replicates per group-1 condition, no
  formal CoV reported beyond the raw pair.
- **Cross-group comparisons are confounded**, not clean: group-2 (128 µm point) was tested
  **13 months before** group-1 (96/168/226 µm), in different ambient conditions, with a
  **different, plain (non-dacro) bolt** — so the 128 µm point is not strictly "one more
  thickness on the same sweep."
- **Temperature/humidity control matters a lot:** the one climate-controlled specimen
  (R2KFP, 65 µm red lead) gave a stable, tight final value (4.71%); the two **ambient**
  125 µm red-lead specimens differed wildly by bolt species alone (32.6% dacro vs. 16.7%
  A490) — the paper attributes this to compounded temperature/humidity fluctuation *and*
  bolt-coating interaction, not thickness (both are nominally the same 125 µm).
- **Internal paper inconsistency found (flag, not silently resolved):** body text §4.4
  states the 128 µm (R2KFZ) loss rate as **"7.3% at 168 hours and 8.3% at 744 hours"**, but
  **Table 2 states 10.7%/12.5%** for the same specimen. My independent pixel-digitization of
  Fig. 11's actual test-result scatter gives 89.7%/88.75% (≈10.3%/11.25% loss) at those two
  times — closer to **Table 2's** figures than to the body text's. Table 2 (and my
  digitization) is treated as authoritative here; the §4.4 text sentence looks like a
  transcription error in the original paper.
- **Eq. 8's printed subscript is also a typo:** it is labeled `Creepstrain₁₆₈` in the PDF but
  is contextually and numerically the **96 µm** case (section header "4.1 Model for 96 µm
  coating," Table 3 is explicitly "t = 96 µm," and the α/β values match Table 7's 96 µm row
  exactly, not the 168 µm row). Resolved here as the 96 µm equation; both readings recorded
  for the record.

## Conclusions (paper's own)

- A quantitative model for relaxation of high-strength bolted joints with inorganic-zinc
  faying-surface coating was established (candidate bolt: F10T M20 dacro-coated TC bolt).
  Loss ranges 10–18% over the 96–226 µm actual-thickness range tested.
  >85% of the 744 h total relaxation occurs within the first 7 days — consistent with
  Kulak et al. (2001).
- Regardless of coating, the creep-strain law simplifies to **`Creep = α + β·log₁₀(t)`**
  (Eq. 16); combined with the bolt/coating compliance ratio it gives `F(t)/F₀`.
- **Practical recommendation:** inorganic zinc on the faying surface should be **≤75–100 µm**
  — even the thinnest tested condition (96 µm) failed the "design load + 10% redundancy"
  slip-critical guideline. The Korean manufacturer's own 75 µm guideline "appears reasonable."
- Red lead paint: the climate-controlled 65 µm specimen was stable at 4.71% loss; the two
  ambient 125 µm specimens differed sharply by bolt species (32.6% vs. 16.7%) — ambient
  temperature/humidity control is recommended as a design/QA consideration for red-lead-paint
  faying surfaces.
- Temperature effects on creep were **explicitly out of scope** ("this study does not
  consider the creep testing at temperature equivalent to the actual temperature of the
  steel in service... further research... shall be needed").

## V2 mapping

- **This paper is a *new pair class* for `C_creep`, not a re-calibration of BAS V2's
  existing thread/bearing `C_creep`.** BAS V2's `C_creep` (default 5e-11, âncora interna-anchored,
  304SS-anchored per `MODEL_LEGITIMACY.md` §4.7) models creep at the **thread/bearing
  contact interface** inside the joint. This paper's creep mechanism is **faying-surface
  coating creep** — a paint/primer film sandwiched **between the two clamped plates**,
  structurally closer to a thin-gasket creep problem than to thread/bearing creep. Do not
  merge this data into the existing `C_creep` anchor set; if BAS V2 ever models
  faying-surface (non-thread) creep explicitly (e.g., for galvanized/painted structural
  splice connections), this is the anchor data for that *separate* mechanism/pair.
- **Strongest L5 evidence supplied: within *one* bolt/coating pair, the effective creep rate
  scales with coating thickness.** `β` (the log-time creep-rate coefficient) rises
  monotonically with actual thickness across the group-1 sweep: 3.0×10⁻⁵ (96 µm) →
  4.0×10⁻⁵ (168 µm) → 4.5×10⁻⁵ (226 µm) — same bolt, same coating species, same test
  batch, only thickness varies. This is a clean, controlled demonstration that "the
  constant" is really a **function of a per-pair geometric/material parameter** (coating
  thickness), reinforcing L5's core thesis (`C_creep` is per-pair, not universal) one level
  deeper than a simple pair-to-pair table.
- **Form-choice note for V2:** this paper's own regression is **logarithmic in time**
  (`Creep = α + β·log₁₀t`), not the Norton power-law form (`∝ tᵐ`) used elsewhere in the
  literature (e.g., the Yang & DeWolf model it cites, Eq. 7, or BAS V2's own creep
  formulation). Both forms are used in the creep literature for ostensibly similar
  bolt-relaxation problems — worth remembering that BAS V2's Norton-power-law choice for
  `C_creep` is a **form assumption**, not the only literature-supported option, if a future
  gate ever falsifies the power-law shape against new data.
- **Order-of-magnitude anchor only:** the 4–18% (inorganic zinc) / 4.7–32.6% (red lead
  paint) preload-loss ranges over ~1 month of static hold are a useful sanity-check envelope
  for "how much can faying-surface creep alone move `F₀`" — useful context when interpreting
  BAS V2 `C_creep`-driven predictions on painted/galvanized structural joints, even though
  the mechanism and functional form differ from BAS V2's own `C_creep`.

## Caveats

- **F₀ (preload) and plate/grip thickness are never stated as numbers** anywhere in the
  extracted text — only bolt grade/class (F10T M20) and the joint's plan-view dimensions.
  Do not backfill a specific kN value into V2 config from this paper; if a preload number is
  needed, it must come from the KS B 2819 / F10T M20 standard proof-load table directly, not
  from this paper (which never restates it).
- **`Cf`, `H₀`, `Ac` (Eq. 4's compliance/modulus/area terms) are not given numerically** for
  these specific specimens — only the general formulas (Eqs. 2, 5, 6). The `_predicted.csv`
  files for **168 µm and 128 µm** were therefore reconstructed **analytically**: I inverted
  Eq. 4/11/15 at several cleanly-digitized (uncontaminated) points to back-solve a single
  lumped constant `k = (H₀/Ac)/Cf` (median over 14–30 points, low scatter — e.g. 168 µm:
  k≈2494, std≈340 excluding one log-sensitive outlier at t=5h; 128 µm: k≈241, std≈12), then
  evaluated the full `F(t)/F₀ = (1+kα)/(1+k(α+β·log₁₀t))` curve on a log-spaced time grid.
  **`k` is my back-calculation, not a value stated in the paper** — treat these two
  `_predicted.csv` files as a faithful reproduction of the *shape* of Eqs. 11/15, not an
  independent physical derivation. The 96 µm and 226 µm `_predicted.csv` files did **not**
  need this treatment — their predicted-line color (red) was cleanly pixel-separable from
  the test-result color throughout, so those two are direct pixel digitizations.
- **Why the analytical fallback was needed for 168/128 µm specifically:** Fig. 7's predicted
  line is **solid black**, which becomes pixel-indistinguishable from the overlapping purple
  "Test Result" trace once the two visually converge (paper's own text: "synchronized...
  200 hours" — after which any dark, low-saturation pixel could belong to either curve).
  Fig. 11's predicted line is a very **faint, low-contrast cyan dash** (near-white in the
  350 dpi raster) that could not be reliably separated from anti-aliasing noise. Fig. 5 (red)
  and Fig. 9 (red vs. maroon, separable via a dual-cluster per-column heuristic since the two
  colors are close but the lines stay visually distinct/ordered in most columns) did not
  have this problem.
- **Digitization accuracy** (see table above): ±0.1–0.2 pp for 96/168/226 µm, up to +1.25 pp
  for 128 µm at the 744 h checkpoint (validated against Table 2's exact values). Treat the
  underlying test-result traces themselves as carrying comparable real sensor noise (ambient
  temperature cycling, explicitly discussed by the authors) — the digitization is not
  materially less precise than the source signal.
- **Two internal paper inconsistencies found and flagged, not silently corrected** (see
  Nuances): (1) §4.4 body text (7.3%/8.3%) vs. Table 2 (10.7%/12.5%) for specimen R2KFZ —
  Table 2 (matching my independent digitization) is treated as authoritative; (2) Eq. 8's
  printed subscript (`₁₆₈`) contextually and numerically belongs to the 96 µm case.
- **Cross-group (96/168/226 µm vs. 128 µm) comparison is confounded** by a 13-month gap,
  different ambient conditions, and a different (non-dacro) bolt — only the group-1
  three-point sweep (96/168/226 µm) is a clean, single-variable thickness sweep.
- **No bare/uncoated-steel specimen and no "8.37%" value exist in this paper.** The task
  brief's hint text ("bare (uncoated) steel ~3% loss," "thin coatings 4.71–8.37%") could
  **not be verified** against Table 1, Table 2, or any figure in this PDF — every specimen
  has a stated coating (inorganic zinc or red lead paint); the closest match is the 65 µm
  red lead paint specimen at 4.71% final loss (the single "4.71" figure does match exactly).
  This looks like a paraphrase possibly drawing on a related-but-different Nah et al. paper
  in the same series (2009a, 2009b, or 2010 — all cited in this paper's own references list
  but not present in this download) rather than an error in this note. No bare-steel CSV is
  provided because no such curve/table exists in the source PDF.
- **Small replicate count:** n=2 per group-1 thickness condition, n=1 per group-2 condition
  — no formal variance/CoV statistic beyond the raw replicate pairs shown in Table 2.
- **Static creep only, no cyclic/vibratory loosening** — this paper contributes nothing
  about BAS V2's wear/embedding/rotational-loosening mechanisms or their transverse-slip
  coupling; it is purely a `C_creep`-class (or new-pair-class) data point.
